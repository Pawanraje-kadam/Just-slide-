# Open-Source Engineering
What Makes an Open-Source Project Sustainable? | Intermediate | Maintenance | Sustainability requires more than code quality: maintainer capacity, funding, release cadence, and a path for new contributors determine whether a project survives. | Critical infrastructure often depends on a few unpaid maintainers. | What would a healthy maintenance plan look like for a project used by millions? | open-source, sustainability, maintenance
Why Is Maintaining Backward Compatibility a Community Decision? | Advanced | Compatibility | In a library, breaking changes affect every downstream user, while not breaking may freeze design. Maintainers must balance evolution against ecosystem trust. | Compatibility policy is part of the public contract, not an internal preference. | How would you stage a breaking change with a deprecation path? | semver, compatibility, community
How Do Licenses Shape Engineering Choices? | Beginner | Licensing | Permissive, copyleft, and source-available licenses impose different obligations on distribution, modification, and linking. These constraints can block or enable adoption. | License compatibility is a legal risk that appears when a dependency is embedded. | What obligations does a copyleft license create for a linked library? | licensing, legal-risk, dependencies
What Makes a Good Maintainer Handoff? | Intermediate | Governance | A handoff needs documented access, release procedures, architecture context, and trusted successors. Without them, projects stall when a maintainer steps away. | Bus factor is a reliability property of the ecosystem, not just the code. | Which credentials and processes should a successor receive? | maintainership, bus-factor, governance
Why Are Transitive Dependencies a Governance Problem? | Advanced | Community | A project you use directly may depend on packages no one on your team has reviewed, with their own maintainers, licenses, and update cadence. | Supply-chain risk grows through depth that package manifests hide. | How would you map and assess the transitive dependency tree of a project? | dependencies, supply-chain, governance
How Does a Contributor Build Trust in a Large Project? | Beginner | Community | Trust is earned through small, reliable contributions, respectful review, and understanding project norms. Access follows demonstrated judgment, not enthusiasm. | Open source is a social system as much as a codebase. | What would a first contribution look like that reduces maintainer review burden? | contribution, trust, community
What Makes an Open-Source Release Policy Clear? | Advanced | Governance | A clear policy states versioning semantics, deprecation windows, supported branches, and security response expectations. Users can then plan upgrades rationally. | Ambiguous release expectations cause ecosystem-wide disruption. | When does a new major version require a migration guide rather than a changelog? | releases, semver, deprecation
Why Is Documentation a Reliability Feature? | Intermediate | Maintenance | Documentation defines intended behavior, failure modes, and operational requirements. Without it, users depend on source archaeology and spread misinformation. | Undocumented systems are effectively unstable even if the code never changes. | Which operational failure mode would you document before writing an example? | documentation, reliability, maintenance
How Do Foundations Change Project Incentives? | Advanced | Governance | Foundations provide legal neutrality, funding, and shared governance, but introduce process and competing stakeholder interests. | Governance structure determines who can direct a project long-term. | What decisions become slower when a project moves into a foundation? | foundations, governance, incentives
