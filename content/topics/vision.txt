# Computer Vision
Why Are Vision Models Fooled by Small Perturbations? | Advanced | Representation Learning | Adversarial examples exploit high-dimensional decision boundaries: tiny, often imperceptible pixel changes can move an input across a class boundary. | Robustness failures matter when perception drives safety or moderation decisions. | How does an adversarial perturbation differ from ordinary image noise? | adversarial-examples, robustness, security
How Does Convolution Exploit Spatial Structure? | Beginner | Image Models | Convolution applies learned filters across local neighborhoods, sharing weights so patterns are detected regardless of position while preserving spatial relationships. | It encodes an assumption about images that makes learning far more efficient than a dense network. | Why does weight sharing reduce parameters for image models? | convolution, spatial-structure, cnn
What Makes Object Detection Different From Classification? | Intermediate | Detection | Detection must decide whether objects exist, where they are, and how confident the model is, then resolve overlapping predictions. | Localization adds geometric ambiguity that classification metrics do not capture. | How does non-maximum suppression resolve duplicate detections? | object-detection, localization, nms
Why Is Data Augmentation Important in Vision? | Beginner | Representation Learning | Augmentation creates transformed copies of training images so the model learns invariances to rotation, crop, color, and lighting that the dataset lacks. | It stretches limited labeled data and reduces overfitting to irrelevant variation. | Which augmentations could change the label and therefore should be avoided? | data-augmentation, invariance, overfitting
How Do Vision Transformers Differ From Convolutions? | Advanced | Image Models | Transformers split images into patches and model global attention between them, losing some built-in locality but gaining flexible long-range interactions. | Architecture choice changes data efficiency, compute cost, and inductive bias. | What inductive bias does a convolution have that a transformer must learn from data? | transformers, convolution, architecture
What Makes Multimodal Alignment Hard? | Advanced | Multimodal Systems | Aligning images and text requires mapping different modalities into a shared space where related concepts are close, despite drastically different statistics and ambiguities. | Multimodal systems are central to search, generation, and accessibility tools. | What kinds of image-text pairs are ambiguous even for a human labeler? | multimodal, alignment, embeddings
Why Does Camera Calibration Matter for Perception? | Advanced | Sensors | Calibration estimates intrinsic and extrinsic parameters so pixels can be related to real-world geometry. Miscalibration silently distorts measurement and fusion. | Robotics and augmented reality depend on accurate geometry, not just labels. | What errors appear when extrinsics drift after a camera is bumped? | calibration, geometry, perception
How Do Metrics Like IoU Hide Failure? | Intermediate | Detection | Intersection over Union measures overlap for a predicted box, but it can look acceptable while missing small objects, misclassifying, or failing at boundaries. | A single aggregate score can conceal important failure modes. | Why does averaging IoU across object sizes favor large objects? | iou, metrics, evaluation
What Makes Video Understanding Harder Than Image Understanding? | Intermediate | Video | Video adds temporal order, motion, and long-range dependencies while multiplying data volume. Models must decide what to retain across frames. | Action recognition, tracking, and video search all depend on temporal reasoning. | How would you represent motion without processing every frame at full resolution? | video, temporal-reasoning, efficiency
