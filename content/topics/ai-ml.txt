Why Do Large Language Models Hallucinate? | Intermediate | Model Behavior | Language models generate plausible text by predicting tokens, not by checking facts. Hallucination emerges from training incentives, missing evidence, and confident decoding. | Understanding failure modes is the first step to designing systems that cite sources, abstain, or verify claims instead of trusting fluent output. | Which part of the generation pipeline introduces factual risk, and where can verification be added? | hallucination, evaluation, generative-ai
How Does Data Leakage Inflate Machine Learning Results? | Intermediate | Evaluation | Leakage happens when training data carries information that will not be available at prediction time, from mistimed features to duplicated rows appearing in both train and test sets. | A model that looks excellent offline but fails in production is often leaking rather than learning. | How would you audit a feature pipeline for information that only exists after the label is known? | data-leakage, evaluation, ml-pipelines
How Do Embedding Models Represent Semantic Similarity? | Intermediate | Representation Learning | Embedding models map text to vectors whose geometry captures learned associations, but proximity reflects training data rather than universal meaning. | Embeddings power search, recommendation, and retrieval-augmented generation, so their blind spots quietly shape what a system can find. | What kinds of relationships does cosine similarity capture poorly? | embeddings, vector-search, representation-learning
Why Can Benchmark Scores Fail to Predict Real-World Performance? | Intermediate | Evaluation | Benchmarks measure narrow tasks under fixed conditions. Real deployment adds new inputs, new users, and new costs, so a high score can overstate usefulness. | Teams that optimize for leaderboard wins can ship models that break on the messy distribution users actually produce. | What would a task-based evaluation include that a static benchmark misses? | benchmarks, evaluation, generalization
What Happens to a Model When the World Changes After Training? | Intermediate | Model Behavior | Distribution shift occurs when deployment inputs drift away from training data. Accuracy decays silently because the model has no built-in signal that the world has changed. | Monitoring for drift is how teams learn that a model is becoming confidently obsolete. | Which signals would reveal a data shift before users report wrong predictions? | distribution-shift, monitoring, ml-systems
Why Is a Calibrated Probability More Useful Than a Confident Prediction? | Advanced | Evaluation | Calibration means predicted probabilities match observed frequencies. A model can be accurate yet badly calibrated, which makes its confidence scores useless for decisions. | Downstream systems act on confidence thresholds, and miscalibration turns those thresholds into arbitrary rules. | How would you measure calibration on a dataset with rare outcomes? | calibration, probabilistic-ml, evaluation
How Do Models Learn Spurious Correlations? | Intermediate | Model Behavior | If a shortcut correlates with labels in training data, a model will happily learn it. Shortcuts survive validation when the test set shares the same accidental pattern. | Spurious features explain many confident failures after deployment and are difficult to spot without designed stress tests. | Why can a held-out test set fail to expose a shortcut that a subgroup split would reveal? | spurious-correlations, robustness, dataset-design
What Makes an AI Evaluation Dataset Trustworthy? | Intermediate | Evaluation | A reliable evaluation set is representative, versioned, free of leaked labels, and maintained as the product changes. Quality control matters as much as sample size. | Without trustworthy evaluation, every improvement claim is a guess dressed up as a metric. | How would you detect contamination between a public benchmark and a model's training data? | evaluation, dataset-quality, benchmarks
Why Do Models Memorize Some Training Examples? | Advanced | Model Behavior | Large models can reproduce rare or duplicated examples instead of learning general patterns. Memorization increases with model capacity and the frequency of outliers. | It connects privacy, copyright, and generalization: a model that memorizes can leak data and fail to reason. | Which dataset properties make memorization more likely, and how could you test for it? | memorization, privacy, generalization
Why Does Data Quality Beat Data Quantity in Many ML Projects? | Intermediate | Applied ML | Noisy labels and duplicated samples teach the wrong lesson, while clean, diverse data can improve results even when the dataset is smaller. | Most applied machine learning gains come from fixing data, not from swapping model architectures. | How could you find and repair the most damaging label errors in a training set? | data-quality, labeling, applied-ml
