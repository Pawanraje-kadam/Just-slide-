# Privacy & Digital Trust
Why Is Anonymized Data Often Re-identifiable? | Intermediate | Anonymity | Removing names is not enough when combinations of age, location, and behavior narrow a person to a unique record. Linkage attacks reconnect anonymized datasets with public information. | Promising anonymity without measuring re-identification risk harms users and violates trust. | How would you estimate the uniqueness of records in a dataset before releasing it? | anonymization, re-identification, data-release
What Makes Data Minimization an Engineering Practice? | Beginner | Data Minimization | Collecting less data reduces breach impact, storage cost, and compliance scope. It requires asking each field whether the feature truly needs it. | Data that is never collected cannot leak or be misused. | Which analytics field in your product could be removed without changing a decision? | data-minimization, privacy-by-design, storage
How Does Differential Privacy Bound Information Leakage? | Advanced | Anonymity | Differential privacy adds calibrated noise so an observer cannot tell whether any individual was in the dataset, with a quantified privacy budget. | It replaces the binary fiction of anonymous versus identified with measurable guarantees. | What does the epsilon parameter trade off against accuracy? | differential-privacy, noise, statistics
Why Are Third-Party Scripts a Privacy Risk? | Beginner | Tracking | Analytics, ads, and support widgets run with access to the page and can collect behavior beyond what the first party intends. Each script expands the trust boundary. | Most sites leak data through code they did not write. | What data could a third-party script read from a checkout page? | third-party, tracking, web-privacy
What Makes Consent Flow Legible to Users? | Intermediate | Consent | Valid consent must be informed, specific, and revocable. Interfaces that bundle purposes or obscure defaults undermine the choice they claim to capture. | Consent is both a legal requirement and a UX design problem. | How would you present two data uses without nudging users toward acceptance? | consent, ux, regulation
How Do Engineers Design for Data Deletion? | Advanced | Data Deletion | Deletion must propagate through caches, backups, analytics copies, and derived datasets while preserving referential integrity. Soft deletes can quietly defeat the promise. | Users and regulators expect deletion to mean deletion, not hiding a row. | How would you locate every copy of a user's data across a system? | deletion, data-lifecycle, compliance
Why Is Telemetry a Privacy Minefield? | Intermediate | Tracking | Logs, traces, and product analytics can capture identifiers, payloads, and sensitive context. Aggregating them later does not undo overcollection. | Observability systems are often the least governed data store in a company. | What fields would you redact before storing an HTTP trace? | telemetry, logs, redaction
What Makes a Privacy Policy Useful to Engineers? | Beginner | Trust Engineering | A policy is useful when it accurately maps to system behavior: what is collected, where it flows, how long it lives, and who can access it. | Mismatch between policy and implementation is a legal and ethical failure. | How would you verify that a policy statement matches the actual data flow? | privacy-policy, data-flow, governance
How Do Fingerprinting Techniques Work? | Advanced | Tracking | Fingerprinting combines attributes such as browser version, fonts, and rendering behavior into a stable identifier that survives cookie deletion. | It circumvents user choice and is difficult to detect from the user side. | Which browser properties are stable enough to identify a device across sessions? | fingerprinting, tracking, browser
