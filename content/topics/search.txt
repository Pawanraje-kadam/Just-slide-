# Information Retrieval & Search
How Does an Inverted Index Support Fast Search? | Beginner | Inverted Indexes | An inverted index maps each term to the documents containing it, so a query looks up term lists and intersects or ranks them instead of scanning every document. | It is the data structure behind most full-text search systems. | How does posting-list compression affect both index size and query speed? | inverted-index, full-text, data-structures
Why Is Precision at K a Misleading Retrieval Metric? | Intermediate | Evaluation | Precision at K rewards whatever fills the top K, even when the ranking order or recall of relevant items is poor. It can hide missed results outside the cutoff. | Teams tune ranking against a metric that does not reflect whether users find what exists. | When would recall at K be more informative than precision at K? | retrieval-metrics, precision, recall
What Makes Hybrid Search Better Than Keyword or Vector Alone? | Advanced | Vector Search | Keyword search excels at exact terms and identifiers, while embeddings capture meaning. Combining scores or reranking can cover both, but fusion weights need evaluation. | Real queries mix semantic intent with precise strings like error codes and names. | How would you merge lexical and vector rankings without letting one dominate? | hybrid-search, bm25, embeddings
How Do Search Engines Rank Results? | Beginner | Ranking | Ranking combines term match, field weights, document quality, freshness, and behavioral signals, often through learned or hand-tuned scoring. | Ranking is where retrieval quality becomes visible to users. | What signals would you use to rank documentation pages for a technical query? | ranking, relevance, search
Why Does Tokenization Change Retrieval Quality? | Intermediate | Query Understanding | Tokenization decides what counts as a term, how compounds split, and whether stemming or Unicode normalization applies. Different choices produce different indexes and matches. | A search system can appear broken simply because the query and document were segmented differently. | Why does language-specific tokenization matter for German compound words? | tokenization, normalization, retrieval
What Makes a Learning-to-Rank Model Risky? | Advanced | Ranking | Learned rankers depend on labeled data and logging policy, so feedback loops, position bias, and distribution shift can reward the wrong results. | Optimizing clicks without causal care can make search worse while metrics improve. | How does position bias contaminate click labels used for ranking? | learning-to-rank, feedback-loops, bias
Why Can Embedding Search Miss Exact Matches? | Intermediate | Vector Search | Embeddings compress meaning into vectors and can blur exact identifiers, rare tokens, and negations. Similarity in vector space is not equality of strings. | RAG and semantic search quietly fail on the queries that need precision most. | How would you design retrieval for a query that is an exact error code? | embeddings, exact-match, retrieval
How Do Search Indexes Stay Fresh? | Intermediate | Inverted Indexes | New documents must be searchable without rebuilding the whole index. Systems use incremental segments, merges, and near-real-time refresh, trading freshness for throughput. | Users lose trust in search when their latest content is invisible. | What does an index refresh actually make visible, and after how long? | indexing, freshness, segments
What Makes a Relevance Judgment Set Reliable? | Advanced | Evaluation | Judgment sets require clear criteria, representative queries, multiple annotators, and measures of agreement. Otherwise evaluation rewards one person's taste. | Search improvements are impossible to verify without a trustworthy notion of relevance. | How would you measure inter-annotator agreement on graded relevance? | relevance-judgments, evaluation, annotation
