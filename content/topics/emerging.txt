# Emerging Computing Paradigms
What Makes Quantum Error Correction Necessary? | Advanced | Quantum Computing | Qubits lose coherence and accumulate errors from their environment. Useful computation requires encoding logical qubits across many physical ones and correcting errors continuously. | Without error correction, quantum advantage remains limited to fragile demonstrations. | Why does correcting a qubit error risk disturbing neighboring qubits? | quantum, error-correction, qubits
How Do Quantum Gates Differ From Classical Logic? | Beginner | Quantum Computing | Quantum gates manipulate amplitudes and entanglement, producing probabilistic measurement outcomes. Classical gates operate on definite bits with deterministic truth tables. | It reframes what a quantum program can and cannot promise. | Why can a quantum algorithm only give probabilistic answers? | quantum-gates, entanglement, computation
What Makes Neuromorphic Computing Different? | Advanced | Neuromorphic Hardware | Neuromorphic chips use event-driven spikes and in-memory computation to mimic neural dynamics, trading general-purpose flexibility for energy efficiency on specific workloads. | It offers a different point in the energy-versus-flexibility design space. | Why are sparse, event-driven signals efficient on neuromorphic hardware? | neuromorphic, spiking, hardware
Why Is Optical Computing Hard to Make Practical? | Advanced | Optical Computing | Light can carry huge bandwidth with low loss, but switching, storing, and converting between optical and electronic domains remain difficult and expensive. | The promise of optical interconnects is more immediate than optical processors. | What prevents an optical signal from being stored as easily as an electrical charge? | optical, photonics, computing
What Makes Edge AI Constrained? | Intermediate | Edge AI | Edge devices have limited memory, power, and thermal headroom, so models must be compressed, quantized, or partitioned while meeting latency and privacy requirements. | Edge inference changes both model design and system architecture. | Which model optimizations preserve accuracy when memory is tight? | edge-ai, quantization, embedded
How Might Post-CMOS Devices Change Software? | Advanced | Post-CMOS | New device physics may trade determinism, speed, or energy in ways that require compilers and programming models to reason about probabilistic errors and different cost structures. | Hardware assumptions are embedded throughout software stacks. | What would software need to change if memory writes were probabilistic? | post-cmos, hardware, programming-models
What Makes Analog Computing Attractive Again? | Advanced | Analog Computing | Analog systems compute with continuous physical quantities, potentially offering high energy efficiency for matrix and differential-equation workloads, at the cost of noise and limited precision. | Some AI workloads tolerate approximate arithmetic better than general programs do. | How would noise and calibration error limit analog computation's precision? | analog-computing, approximation, hardware
Why Is DNA Storage Interesting for Archives? | Intermediate | DNA Storage | DNA can store extremely dense, long-lived information with random access achievable through encoding and sequencing. Read and write latency remain high. | Archival workloads value density and longevity over interactive access. | What encoding constraints must DNA storage satisfy to be reliable? | dna-storage, archival, storage
Why Does Superconducting Logic Require Cryogenic Infrastructure? | Advanced | Post-CMOS | Superconducting circuits operate at temperatures near absolute zero, where materials lose resistance. The cooling plant becomes a dominant cost and complexity. | Any performance gain must be weighed against a facility that is radically harder to operate. | What fraction of a superconducting computer's energy budget goes to refrigeration? | superconducting, cryogenics, infrastructure
