# Datacenter & Infrastructure Engineering

How Does Cooling Limit Compute Density? | Intermediate | Cooling | Racks generate concentrated heat that must be removed within temperature limits. Airflow, coolant, and layout determine how much compute fits in a space. | Cooling constraints shape where GPUs can be deployed and how expensive they are to run. | Why does liquid cooling enable denser GPU racks than air cooling? | cooling, density, infrastructure
What Makes a Network Fabric a Bottleneck? | Advanced | Network Fabric | Large clusters need high bisection bandwidth, low latency, and non-blocking paths. Oversubscription and congestion can leave expensive compute waiting on data. | Distributed training and storage performance depend on the fabric as much as the servers. | What is bisection bandwidth and why does it matter for training clusters? | network-fabric, bandwidth, clusters
Why Is Power Usage Effectiveness a Limited Metric? | Intermediate | Power | PUE measures facility overhead but not how useful the computing work is. A highly efficient datacenter can still waste energy on idle or inefficient workloads. | Efficiency claims need to include utilization and workload value, not just facility ratios. | What does a low PUE hide about actual computing efficiency? | pue, efficiency, sustainability
How Do Hardware Failures Scale With Fleet Size? | Advanced | Hardware Lifecycle | Even a low annual failure rate produces frequent failures across thousands of machines. Fleet operations must assume constant replacement and graceful degradation. | At scale, individual failures are routine events that software must absorb. | How does fleet size change the expected number of failures per day? | failures, fleet, reliability
What Makes Workload Placement Hard? | Advanced | Capacity | Placement must balance power, cooling, latency, failure domains, hardware type, and data locality. Optimal placement on one dimension can violate another. | Efficient clusters depend on scheduling decisions that are far from trivial. | Which constraints would prevent an otherwise idle GPU from accepting a job? | scheduling, placement, capacity
Why Are GPUs Harder to Cool Than CPUs? | Intermediate | Cooling | GPUs concentrate high power in dense packages and often run sustained workloads, producing heat that air cooling struggles to remove from a small area. | The thermal profile of accelerators shapes datacenter design and cost. | What changes when a rack moves from 10 kW to 100 kW of compute? | gpu, cooling, thermal
