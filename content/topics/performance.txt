# Performance Engineering
Why Should You Profile Before You Optimize? | Beginner | Profiling | Intuition about where time goes is frequently wrong. Profilers attribute CPU time, allocations, and waits to code paths, exposing the actual bottleneck. | Optimization without measurement mostly adds complexity. | What kind of profiler would you use to find time spent waiting on I/O? | profiling, measurement, optimization
What Is Tail Latency and Why Does It Dominate User Experience? | Intermediate | Latency Analysis | Averages hide slow requests. A user waiting on many parallel calls experiences the slowest one, so p95 and p99 latency often predict frustration better than the mean. | Systems that look fast on average can still feel slow to real users. | Why does a page that fans out to twenty services experience the tail of each? | tail-latency, percentiles, user-experience
Why Does Lock Contention Collapse Throughput? | Intermediate | Throughput | When many threads need the same lock, they serialize, context-switch, and waste CPU waiting. Adding threads can reduce total progress despite more concurrency on paper. | Contention turns parallelism into coordination overhead. | How would you measure whether a lock is the bottleneck rather than the work it protects? | lock-contention, concurrency, throughput
How Do You Build a Benchmark That Is Not Misleading? | Advanced | Benchmarking | A benchmark must control inputs, warm-up, environment, and measurement scope, then report variance rather than a single number. Small changes can reverse conclusions. | Teams make architectural decisions from benchmarks that measure the wrong thing. | What sources of variance would you report alongside a throughput result? | benchmarking, methodology, measurement
What Makes Memory Allocation a Performance Concern? | Intermediate | Memory Behavior | Allocation and deallocation cost time, fragment the heap, and trigger garbage collection. Reducing allocation can improve both throughput and latency. | Hot paths often spend more time managing memory than doing useful computation. | How would you find an allocation hotspot in a service? | allocation, memory, profiling
How Does CPU Frequency Scaling Affect Software Performance? | Advanced | Profiling | Processors adjust frequency and power based on load and thermal limits, so identical code runs at different speeds across cores and moments. Turbo behavior complicates benchmarking. | Results from a quiet laptop may not transfer to a busy server. | Why can a multi-threaded benchmark show lower per-core speed than a single-threaded one? | frequency-scaling, benchmarking, hardware
Why Is the Critical Path the Only Path That Matters? | Advanced | Latency Analysis | Total latency is determined by the longest chain of dependent work, not the sum or average of all tasks. Parallel work off the critical path does not reduce response time. | Optimizing tasks that are not on the critical path produces no visible improvement. | What tasks in a request would you parallelize to shorten its response time? | critical-path, latency, dependencies
What Makes a Flame Graph Useful? | Beginner | Profiling | A flame graph stacks sampled call frames to show where time accumulates, with width representing frequency and depth representing call relationships. | It turns a profile into a visual map of hot paths and their callers. | How do you read a wide frame that is not a leaf in a flame graph? | flame-graphs, profiling, visualization
When Is Caching a Performance Anti-Pattern? | Intermediate | Throughput | Caching adds memory, invalidation, and consistency costs. If the hit rate is low or the data changes constantly, the cache can make the system slower and harder to reason about. | A cache is a bet on reuse, and some workloads do not justify it. | What hit rate would a cache need before it pays for its complexity? | caching, trade-offs, performance
