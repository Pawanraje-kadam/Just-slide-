# Site Reliability Engineering
How Do SLOs, SLIs, and Error Budgets Influence Decisions? | Intermediate | SLOs | SLIs measure user-visible behavior, SLOs set a target, and error budgets turn the remaining allowed failure into a shared decision tool for velocity versus reliability. | They replace arguments about perfection with explicit trade-offs about risk. | What SLI would represent the experience of a user loading your checkout page? | slo, sli, error-budget
Why Is Mean Time to Recover a Better Metric Than Mean Time Between Failures? | Beginner | Incident Response | MTBF rewards avoiding incidents, while MTTR measures how quickly service is restored. Complex systems will fail, so recovery capability matters more. | Teams can improve availability by recovering faster even if incidents remain frequent. | What parts of your incident response path are untested in practice? | mttr, reliability, incidents
What Makes an Incident Postmortem Useful? | Intermediate | Incident Response | A useful postmortem reconstructs the timeline, identifies contributing conditions, and produces specific follow-up actions without blaming individuals. | Learning depends on surfacing the systemic reasons that made an error likely and damaging. | How would you separate contributing causes from a single root cause narrative? | postmortems, learning, incidents
Why Does Alert Fatigue Destroy On-Call Reliability? | Beginner | Alerting | Too many noisy or non-actionable alerts train responders to ignore pages. Real incidents then compete with routine noise for attention. | An alert that does not require action is a reliability liability. | Which of your alerts would you delete if you required every page to be actionable? | alerting, on-call, fatigue
Why Are Retries Dangerous Without Backoff and Jitter? | Intermediate | Resilience | Retries amplify load during failure. Synchronized retries create waves that keep a struggling service down, while backoff and jitter spread attempts over time. | Retry behavior can turn a brief blip into a cascading outage. | How would you add jitter to a retry schedule without violating latency budgets? | retries, backoff, jitter
What Does a Circuit Breaker Protect? | Intermediate | Resilience | A circuit breaker stops sending requests to a failing dependency after a threshold, allowing it time to recover and freeing callers to fail fast or fall back. | Without it, callers keep consuming resources waiting on a dependency that cannot respond. | When should a circuit breaker move from open back to half-open? | circuit-breaker, resilience, dependencies
Why Is Observability More Than Logging? | Intermediate | Observability | Observability is the ability to ask new questions of a running system using correlated metrics, traces, and structured events, not merely to collect lots of logs. | Teams with high log volume can still be blind to the interaction that caused an outage. | What question about latency would need a trace rather than a metric? | observability, tracing, metrics
What Makes a Capacity Plan Wrong? | Advanced | Capacity Planning | Capacity depends on traffic mix, efficiency, headroom, failure domains, and growth that rarely follows a straight line. Plans based on average load miss peak and failure conditions. | Underprovisioning causes outages; overprovisioning quietly burns money and focus. | How would you model capacity for the loss of one availability zone? | capacity-planning, headroom, reliability
How Do You Debug a System You Cannot Reproduce? | Advanced | Incident Response | Production-only failures require inference from partial traces, sampling, and comparative evidence. Reproducing means reconstructing the relevant state, not replaying every detail. | The most valuable debugging skill is forming hypotheses that can be falsified with available signals. | Which signal would distinguish a race from a resource-exhaustion issue? | debugging, production, traces
