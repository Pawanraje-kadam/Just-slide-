# Technical Product Thinking
How Does a Product Metric Mislead Engineering Decisions? | Intermediate | Metrics | A metric is a proxy, and optimizing it directly can degrade the outcome it was meant to represent. Goodhart's law appears whenever teams treat a proxy as the goal. | Local optimization on a bad metric can make the product worse while dashboards improve. | What behavior would users adopt if they wanted to maximize this metric? | metrics, goodharts-law, product
Why Is Scoping the Hardest Part of Shipping? | Beginner | Scoping | Scope determines what a feature promises, what it omits, and what can be learned. Ambiguity expands work quietly until the launch slips. | Most delays come from unclear boundaries, not slow typing. | What is the smallest version of this feature that would teach you something? | scoping, shipping, delivery
What Makes a Feature Discoverable? | Beginner | Discovery | Discovery is the distance between a user's need and their awareness of the capability. Placement, naming, timing, and onboarding all affect whether it is found. | An unused feature is indistinguishable from one that does not exist. | How would a new user discover this feature without reading a changelog? | discoverability, ux, product
How Should Engineers Talk to Users? | Intermediate | Discovery | Direct conversations reveal workarounds and unmet needs that analytics cannot explain. Good questions ask about past behavior rather than hypothetical preferences. | Engineering decisions improve when builders hear the actual problem in the user's words. | What recent example would you ask a user to walk through step by step? | user-research, discovery, engineering
Why Can a Technically Correct Feature Fail? | Intermediate | Metrics | A feature can solve the stated problem while being too slow, confusing, or costly to use. Technical correctness is necessary but not sufficient for a product outcome. | Teams that stop at passing tests miss whether the solution fits the workflow. | Which part of the user's real workflow would your feature interrupt? | product, correctness, adoption
What Makes Product-Market Fit Observable? | Advanced | Product-Market Fit | Fit shows up as retention, organic growth, and users working around limitations to keep using the product. Surveys alone are weak evidence. | Misreading fit leads teams to scale a product nobody keeps using. | Which retention pattern would distinguish curiosity from genuine demand? | product-market-fit, retention, growth
How Do You Decide What Not to Build? | Intermediate | Prioritization | Saying no requires comparing opportunity cost, maintenance burden, and strategic fit. Every accepted feature becomes permanent surface area. | The cost of a feature is not the build; it is the years of support. | Which existing feature would you remove to make room for this one? | prioritization, opportunity-cost, focus
Why Does Onboarding Determine Retention? | Beginner | Discovery | The first session establishes whether users reach value before encountering friction. Clear first steps and fast wins predict later retention. | Acquisition spend is wasted when first-session friction prevents users from ever reaching the value they signed up for. | What is the shortest path from sign-up to the first meaningful outcome? | onboarding, activation, retention
What Makes a Pricing Model Align With Costs? | Advanced | Prioritization | Usage-based, seat-based, and tiered pricing distribute value and cost differently. Misalignment causes either margin collapse or users avoiding the product. | Pricing shapes architecture by rewarding or penalizing expensive usage patterns. | Which resource costs would you meter to keep pricing sustainable? | pricing, costs, business-model
