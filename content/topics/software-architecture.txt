# Software Architecture
Why Do Abstractions Sometimes Make Software Harder to Maintain? | Intermediate | Modularity | An abstraction hides details, but leaking or unnecessary layers force readers to jump across files and reason about indirection that adds no reusable value. | Every abstraction is a bet that its hidden detail will stay irrelevant. | When does an abstraction earn its indirection cost, and when is it premature? | abstraction, maintainability, design
How Does Dependency Inversion Reduce Coupling? | Intermediate | Modularity | Depending on interfaces rather than concrete implementations lets high-level policy stay stable while low-level details change. | It is the difference between a codebase you can test and one where every change ripples outward. | What makes an interface boundary meaningful rather than a thin wrapper? | dependency-inversion, coupling, design
Why Can Microservices Make a Small Team Less Productive? | Beginner | Service Boundaries | Distributed services add network calls, deployment pipelines, observability, and partial failure handling. A small team pays these costs without enough parallelism to benefit. | Architecture must fit team size and operational maturity, not just scale aspirations. | Which costs of microservices appear immediately even at low traffic? | microservices, team-topology, complexity
Why Does a Shared Database Couple Services? | Intermediate | Service Boundaries | When multiple services read and write the same tables, schema changes and transactional assumptions become cross-team negotiations. The database becomes an undeclared public API. | Shared data is one of the fastest ways to lose independent deployability. | What would it take to move ownership of a table from one service to another safely? | coupling, databases, boundaries
How Should Engineers Choose Between Event-Driven and Request-Driven Designs? | Advanced | Event-Driven Design | Events decouple producers from consumers and absorb bursts, but they make flows harder to trace and duplicate delivery inevitable. Requests are simpler when the caller needs an immediate answer. | The choice changes debugging, consistency, and failure handling for years. | When does an event-driven flow reduce coupling, and when does it merely hide it? | event-driven, messaging, trade-offs
Why Does Technical Debt Compound? | Beginner | Technical Debt | Shortcuts accumulate interest through slower changes, duplicated workarounds, and rising fear of modification. Each workaround makes the next cleanup more expensive. | Debt is a planning concern, not a moral failing; unmanaged interest eventually dominates delivery. | How would you make the cost of a shortcut visible before it compounds? | technical-debt, maintenance, planning
What Makes a Module Boundary Stable Over Time? | Advanced | Evolution | Stable boundaries align with real domain concepts and hide decisions likely to change. Boundaries built around incidental structure become brittle as requirements evolve. | Good boundaries absorb change; bad ones transmit it. | Which decisions inside a module should never leak through its interface? | modularity, boundaries, evolution
How Do Architectural Decision Records Preserve Context? | Beginner | Technical Debt | An ADR records the problem, options, decision, and consequences at a point in time. It preserves reasoning that code and commit history rarely capture. | Teams repeat debates and reverse decisions when the original constraints are forgotten. | What belongs in an ADR that would not be obvious from the resulting code? | adr, documentation, decisions
Why Is Premature Generalization a Form of Technical Debt? | Intermediate | Modularity | Building for imagined future cases adds configuration and indirection that current requirements do not justify. The extra flexibility is rarely exercised or tested. | Speculative generality makes code harder now to help a future that may never arrive. | How can you tell the difference between a real extension point and speculative generality? | overengineering, yagni, design
