# System Design
How Do You Choose Between SQL and a Document Store? | Beginner | Storage Choices | Relational stores excel at joins, constraints, and transactions; document stores favor flexible nested shapes and horizontal partitioning. Access patterns should drive the choice. | The data model is harder to change than the database itself. | Which queries in your product require multi-entity transactions? | sql, document-store, data-modeling
What Makes a Cache Tier Worth the Complexity? | Intermediate | Trade-off Analysis | A cache helps when reads repeat and the source is slow or expensive. It adds consistency windows, invalidation work, and a new failure mode. | Caching is often reached for before the access pattern justifies it. | What read pattern would make a cache almost useless? | caching, trade-offs, architecture
How Do You Design for a Tenfold Traffic Spike? | Intermediate | Scalability | Spikes stress queueing, connection limits, hot keys, and downstream dependencies. Headroom, load shedding, and horizontal scaling each address different bottlenecks. | Systems fail at the component least able to absorb the burst, not uniformly. | Which component would saturate first if traffic grew tenfold tomorrow? | scalability, spikes, load-shedding
Why Does a Shared Queue Help and Hurt Scaling? | Advanced | Scalability | A queue decouples producers from consumers and absorbs bursts, but it can become a bottleneck, reorder work, and hide failures behind growing lag. | Async architecture moves failure into a place teams sometimes forget to monitor. | What would you alert on to detect that queue lag is becoming an outage? | queues, async, scaling
What Makes a Search System Different From a Database? | Intermediate | Storage Choices | Search systems optimize for ranked retrieval over text, with inverted indexes and relevance scoring rather than transactional consistency and arbitrary queries. | Using a database for search or a search engine as a source of truth creates predictable pain. | Which features of a search engine make it a poor system of record? | search, databases, architecture
How Do You Estimate Capacity Before Building? | Beginner | Capacity | Capacity estimation combines expected traffic, request cost, data growth, and peak multipliers into rough resource budgets. The goal is order-of-magnitude sanity, not precision. | Early estimates expose designs that are off by orders of magnitude before they are expensive to change. | What peak-to-average multiplier would you use for a consumer product? | capacity, estimation, planning
Why Do Read and Write Paths Need Different Designs? | Intermediate | Trade-off Analysis | Reads often tolerate caching, replication, and denormalization, while writes must preserve ordering, durability, and contention control. A single design optimizes neither. | Most systems are read-heavy or write-heavy, and pretending otherwise wastes effort. | Which optimizations would help reads but damage write consistency? | read-write, architecture, consistency
What Makes a Multi-Tenant System Hard? | Advanced | Architecture Reviews | Tenants share infrastructure but need isolation, fair resource use, per-tenant limits, and data separation. Noisy neighbors and cross-tenant leaks are design risks. | SaaS economics depend on sharing, while trust depends on isolation. | How would you prevent one tenant's traffic from degrading another's latency? | multi-tenancy, isolation, saas
When Should You Split a Monolith? | Advanced | Architecture Reviews | Splitting makes sense when independent scaling, ownership, or release cadence justify the operational cost of distribution. Size alone is not a reason. | Premature decomposition trades local complexity for distributed complexity. | Which force in your team would improve if a module became a separate service? | monolith, microservices, decomposition
