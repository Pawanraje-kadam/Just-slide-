How Does Interrupt Latency Affect Reliability? | Advanced | Real-Time Systems | Interrupts pause normal execution and run handlers with tight timing requirements. Long handlers, disabled interrupts, or priority inversion can delay critical work. | Missed interrupts create sensor loss and control instability. | What could cause a high-priority interrupt to wait behind lower-priority work? | interrupts, latency, real-time
Why Is Floating-Point Dangerous in Embedded Control? | Intermediate | Control | Floating-point arithmetic has rounding differences across implementations and can be slow without hardware support. Control logic may assume precision that is not guaranteed. | Determinism matters more than mathematical convenience in safety-critical loops. | Why are fixed-point representations often preferred in control firmware? | floating-point, control, fixed-point
What Makes Firmware Updates Risky? | Advanced | Firmware | A failed update can brick a device with no user-recoverable path. Safe updates need verification, rollback slots, and power-loss resilience. | The fleet is only as recoverable as its update mechanism. | How would you design an update that survives a power loss mid-write? | firmware, ota, recovery
How Do Sensors Introduce Uncertainty? | Beginner | Sensors | Sensor readings combine noise, bias, calibration error, and sampling delay. Software that treats readings as ground truth builds control on shaky foundations. | Perception and control quality start with understanding measurement limits. | How would you combine two noisy sensors into a better estimate? | sensors, noise, estimation
How Does a Watchdog Timer Recover a Stuck Device? | Beginner | Firmware | A watchdog expects periodic resets; if software hangs, the timer reboots the system. Correct use requires resets from healthy execution, not from a timer independent of the hang. | Remote devices need a way to recover without a human power cycle. | Why can feeding a watchdog from an interrupt mask a stuck main loop? | watchdog, recovery, firmware
Why Do Embedded Systems Often Avoid Dynamic Allocation? | Intermediate | Firmware | Heap allocation can fragment memory, fail at unpredictable times, and introduce timing uncertainty. Static or pool allocation makes memory behavior analyzable. | Deterministic resource use is a safety property in constrained devices. | How would you replace frequent dynamic allocation in a hot control path? | memory, embedded, determinism
What Makes a Robotics Control Loop Stable? | Advanced | Control | Stability depends on sampling rate, sensor delay, actuator dynamics, and controller gains.
