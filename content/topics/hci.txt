# Human–Computer Interaction
Why Does Recognition Beat Recall in Interface Design? | Beginner | Cognition | People recognize options more easily than they remember commands or hidden rules. Showing relevant choices reduces memory burden and errors. | Interfaces that require memorization exclude casual and new users. | Where does your interface require users to remember something it could display? | recognition, memory, usability
How Should an Interface Help Users Recover From an Error? | Beginner | Usability | A useful error explains what happened, why, and what the user can do next, near the point of failure. Blame and opaque codes add frustration. | Errors are a high-stakes moment for trust and task completion. | How would you rewrite a generic validation error to guide recovery? | errors, messaging, usability
How Do Accessibility Constraints Improve Design? | Intermediate | Accessibility | Designing for keyboard, screen reader, reduced motion, and low vision often clarifies structure and reduces dependence on fragile visual cues. | Accessibility improvements frequently benefit every user. | What happens to your interface if color and hover are unavailable? | accessibility, inclusive-design, constraints
Why Is Keyboard Navigation Essential? | Beginner | Accessibility | Keyboard users include power users, assistive technology users, and people without precise pointer control. Focus order and visible focus states make navigation possible. | An interface that only works with a mouse excludes users and fails standards. | What should happen when a user presses Tab inside a complex widget? | keyboard, focus, accessibility
What Makes a Dark Mode Not Just Inverted Colors? | Beginner | Interaction Design | Dark themes need different surface elevation, contrast targets, and saturation. Simply flipping luminance produces glare, muddy shadows, and unreadable accents. | Theme quality affects comfort during long sessions. | Why do shadow-based depth cues fail on near-black backgrounds? | dark-mode, color, design-systems
How Do You Measure Usability Without Leading Users? | Intermediate | Evaluation | Usability tests should observe real tasks, avoid steering participants, and capture failures and confusion rather than only satisfaction ratings. | Leading questions produce praise instead of insight. | How would you word a task prompt without revealing the intended path? | usability-testing, research, bias
Why Is Animation Sometimes Harmful? | Intermediate | Interaction Design | Motion can communicate change, but it can also delay tasks, distract, and trigger vestibular discomfort. Reduced-motion support is a functional requirement. | Decorative motion should never compete with content or accessibility. | Which animations would you remove for users who prefer reduced motion? | animation, accessibility, motion
What Makes a Form Easy to Complete? | Beginner | Usability | Clear labels, sensible grouping, inline validation, and minimal required fields reduce errors. Ambiguous formatting and late validation force rework. | Forms are where users abandon products and blame themselves. | When should validation feedback appear during form completion? | forms, validation, ux
How Do Cognitive Load and Interface Density Interact? | Advanced | Cognition | Every visible element competes for attention. Dense interfaces can be efficient for experts but overwhelming for novices; progressive disclosure manages the trade-off. | Design must account for expertise and task urgency, not just visual cleanliness. | Which information could be hidden until a user asks for it? | cognitive-load, information-architecture, ux
