# Engineering Economics & Trade-Offs
Why Can a Cheaper Architecture Cost More at Scale? | Intermediate | Cost Modeling | A design that is cheap at low volume may require manual operations, duplicated work, or excessive transfers as usage grows. Cost structures change nonlinearly with scale. | Architecture decisions should be evaluated at the scale the product expects to reach. | Which cost in your system grows faster than linearly with users? | cost-modeling, scaling, architecture
How Do Engineers Evaluate Build Versus Buy? | Beginner | Build vs Buy | Building gives control and differentiation but consumes engineering capacity forever. Buying is faster but adds vendor dependence, cost, and limited adaptability. | The real comparison includes maintenance and opportunity cost, not just initial price. | What would your team stop working on to build this capability? | build-vs-buy, opportunity-cost, vendors
Why Is Premature Optimization an Economic Error? | Intermediate | Opportunity Cost | Optimization consumes engineering time whose value depends on actual impact. Without measurement, effort often goes to code that is not on the critical path. | Engineering time is the scarcest resource in most teams. | How would you decide whether a ten percent latency improvement is worth a month of work? | optimization, opportunity-cost, engineering
When Does Horizontal Scaling Stop Making Economic Sense? | Advanced | Scaling Economics | Adding machines helps until coordination, data transfer, and operational complexity dominate the cost of the work being distributed. | Not every bottleneck is solved by more capacity. | Which costs grow with the number of nodes rather than the amount of useful work? | scaling, distributed-systems, cost
How Do Cloud Reserved Commitments Change Decisions? | Intermediate | Cost Modeling | Reserved and committed-use discounts lower unit prices in exchange for lock-in and forecasting risk. The effective saving depends on utilization and workload stability. | Cloud pricing is a portfolio decision, not a monthly bill. | What utilization rate makes a reserved commitment cheaper than on-demand? | cloud-cost, commitments, forecasting
Why Do Team Communication Costs Grow With Organization Size? | Intermediate | Team Topology | The number of communication paths grows roughly quadratically with team count, so coordination overhead can outpace individual productivity. | Organizational design is an architectural force. | How would you structure teams to reduce necessary cross-team communication? | team-topology, scaling, communication
What Makes a Migration Cost Estimate Unreliable? | Advanced | Opportunity Cost | Migrations uncover hidden dependencies, undocumented behavior, and data quality problems. Optimistic estimates assume the current system is better understood than it is. | Underestimating migrations traps teams in multi-year projects with unclear value. | What unknown about the existing system would most affect the migration timeline? | migration, estimation, risk
Why Are Developer Minutes the Wrong Productivity Metric? | Intermediate | Team Topology | Time spent typing or closing tickets says nothing about delivered value, unblocked colleagues, or avoided incidents. Output metrics invite gaming and measurement distortion. | Productivity metrics shape behavior, often in unintended directions. | Which invisible work would a ticket-count metric ignore? | productivity, metrics, teams
How Does Opportunity Cost Shape Architecture? | Advanced | Cost Modeling | Every architectural choice consumes future capacity to change. Choosing a complex design now can cost the option to build something else later. | The most expensive architectural decisions are the ones that remove options. | Which future change would this design make difficult or impossible? | opportunity-cost, architecture, strategy
